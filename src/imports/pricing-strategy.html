Pricing Strategy

Delt Pricing Architecture

BCG-style framework for pricing across four product lines. Built on competitive intelligence from Square, Toast, Stripe, and Shopify. Designed to maximize lead capture at the top while protecting margins as merchants scale.

The Strategic Thesis

The merchant services industry is converging on a single insight: the free tier is the new sales team. Square's $0/month plan processes billions. Toast gives away $799 hardware to lock in processing revenue. Shopify offers 3 months at $1. The pattern is clear — give away the wedge, monetize the ecosystem.

For Delt, the wedge is the website. It's tangible on day one, creates switching costs immediately, and opens the door to payments, capital, and Lens. Your pricing should be designed to make saying "yes" to the website effortless, then expand revenue per merchant through progressive feature unlocking and cross-product adoption.

The three tiers below aren't just price points — they're stages of a relationship. Starter is the handshake. Growth is commitment. Scale is partnership.

Why we dropped Growth from $124 → $99 and Scale from $249 → $199

$99 is the psychological threshold where SMBs stop deliberating. At $124, you're in "let me think about it" territory — you're above Shopify Basic ($39), above Square Plus ($49), but not clearly delivering 2–3x the value of either. At $99, you're under $100/mo, which triggers a completely different purchase psychology. The lost $25/mo is recovered 4x over by conversion rate improvement and faster time-to-processing-revenue. Same logic at Scale: $199 keeps you under the $200 barrier while $249 puts you in "I need to run this by my partner" territory.

The real revenue model: SaaS fees are the floor, not the ceiling. At $50K/month in card volume on Growth, processing alone generates ~$1,250/month in gross revenue — that's 12x the subscription fee. The tiers exist to acquire and retain; processing and capital are where the margin lives.

Open Questions

Decisions That Need Locking Down

1. Cash discount / dual pricing on Starter?

Your current site mentions "zero processing fees forever (cash discount)" on Starter. This is a strong differentiator for brick-and-mortar — but it also caps your revenue per merchant on the free tier. Consider whether this applies to all transactions or only in-person, and whether it's a true cash discount program or surcharging (different regulatory requirements).

2. Annual vs. monthly commitment for hardware subsidies

The free wireless terminal at Growth and free POS at Scale are major value signals, but they need to be protected by commitment. Recommend 12-month minimum on Growth and 24-month on Scale for hardware inclusion — otherwise monthly churn eats the hardware cost in 3 months.

3. Lens AI — metered or unlimited?

If Lens queries hit an LLM API (Claude, etc.), there's a real cost-per-query. Consider whether Growth gets a generous monthly allotment (e.g., 100 "Ask Lens" queries/mo) vs. unlimited at Scale. This protects margin while still feeling generous.

4. Capital pre-qualification as conversion trigger

Showing Starter merchants a pre-qualified amount ("You could access up to $25K") that requires Growth upgrade to unlock is powerful — but you need to decide whether this pre-qual is real (soft pull from Plaid CRA data) or indicative (based on self-reported revenue). Real is more credible but costs more per lead.
