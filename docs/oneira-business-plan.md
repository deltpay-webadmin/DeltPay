# Oneira — Engineered Dreams

**Business plan & Shark Tank pitch brief**

## 1. Executive summary

Oneira is a consumer sleep-wellness brand that sells the first mainstream product for **dream design**: a bedside device and app that use scent and audio cues, timed to your sleep stages, to influence *what you dream about*.

Every sleep company on the market optimizes the eight hours you're unconscious for one metric — rest. Nobody addresses the experience inside those hours, even though the average person spends roughly **two hours a night dreaming** — six years of their life. Oneira turns that dead airtime into a product category.

The system has three parts:

1. **Oneira Halo** — a bedside device (scent atomizer + speaker + sonar-based sleep-stage sensing, no wearable required) that releases micro-doses of fragrance and soft audio cues at the right moments of the sleep cycle.
2. **Dream Pods** — swappable scent cartridges, each paired with a pre-sleep audio "incubation" session in the app. Launch themes: *Ocean*, *Forest*, *Flight*, *Childhood Kitchen*, *Aurora*, *Deep Focus* (for next-day creativity).
3. **The Oneira app** — guided targeted-dream-incubation sessions before bed, and an AI dream journal in the morning that transcribes, tags, and tracks your dream life over time.

The business model is razor-and-blades: the Halo starter kit sells for **$189 at 84% gross margin**, and the Dream Pod subscription ($19/month) runs at **~75% gross margin** with a 55% attach rate. Blended gross margin is **~81%** — supplement-grade margins on a hardware product, because the "hardware" is diffuser-class electronics, not a smartwatch.

**The ask: $500,000 for 10% equity** ($5M valuation), funding inventory for the first retail purchase orders and the growth marketing engine behind a category we get to name.

## 2. The problem

- **Sleep is a solved conversation; dreams are not.** Consumers spent an estimated $30B+ on sleep tech and sleep aids last year — trackers, smart mattresses, sound machines, melatonin. All of it competes on the same promise (fall asleep faster, wake up fresher). None of it touches the content of sleep itself.
- **People already want this and have nowhere to spend money on it.** The lucid-dreaming community is one of the internet's most durable obsessions (the largest subreddit alone has over half a million members), "dream interpretation" sustains millions of monthly searches, and restless sleepers with recurring stress dreams have no consumer product aimed at them at all.
- **The tools people improvise are terrible.** Dream journals on paper, reality-check phone alarms, YouTube binaural beats. There is demand, community, and ritual — but no hero product. That is what a category vacuum looks like.

## 3. The science (and how we talk about it)

Oneira is an engineering wrapper around two well-replicated findings from sleep research:

- **Sensory cues during sleep reach the dreaming brain.** Odors presented during sleep influence memory consolidation and dream emotion — smell is the only sense with a direct anatomical shortcut to the brain's emotion and memory centers, and unlike sound, scent does not wake the sleeper. (Rasch et al., *Science*, 2007 — odor cues during slow-wave sleep; Schredl et al., 2009 — positive vs. negative odors shifting the emotional tone of dreams.)
- **Dream content can be steered at sleep onset.** MIT Media Lab's *Dormio* project (Horowitz et al., 2020) demonstrated **targeted dream incubation (TDI)**: repeated audio prompts during the hypnagogic period reliably inserted chosen themes into reported dreams. Carr et al.'s 2020 review in *Consciousness and Cognition* frames the emerging field of "dream engineering."

Oneira's protocol pairs both mechanisms: a pre-sleep audio incubation session plants the theme, then the Halo re-cues the paired scent during late-night REM windows, when dreaming is longest and most vivid.

**Positioning discipline (important):** Oneira is a wellness product, marketed like aromatherapy and meditation apps — *"designed to inspire the dreams you want,"* never *"treats"* anything. No medical claims, no PTSD/nightmare-disorder claims, no sleep-disorder claims. Fragrances are IFRA-compliant and cool-air atomized (no heat, no combustion), the same regulatory posture as any premium diffuser brand. This keeps us squarely outside FDA medical-device territory and is exactly how Calm, Headspace, and Hatch built nine-figure businesses.

Honest limitation, stated plainly (Sharks will ask): dream incubation is probabilistic, not deterministic. The published TDI work shows reliable *thematic influence*, not scripted dreams. Our product promise is calibrated to that: prime the theme, boost dream recall, and make the whole ritual delightful. The AI dream journal closes the loop — users *see* their hit rate, which is itself the retention hook.

- **Year 1:** Halo + 6 launch pod themes + app (iOS/Android). Limited-drop collab pods (artists, composers) to drive re-purchase among non-subscribers.
- **Year 2:** *Duet* mode (two sleepers, one device, per-side scent timing), themed seasonal drops, dream-journal social features (share a dream card, not raw data).
- **Year 3:** Halo 2 with in-array temperature and light cueing; licensed "dreamworld" pods (film/game IP is a natural fit — fall asleep into a franchise).

**Moat:** (1) utility patent filing on sleep-stage-synchronized olfactory release with closed-loop cue scheduling; (2) proprietary pod dock — the cartridge ecosystem is ours; (3) the dream-outcome dataset: millions of journaled dreams tagged against the cues that preceded them, compounding into the best cue-to-dream model in existence; (4) the brand that names the category. Fast followers can copy a diffuser; they can't copy the dataset or the community.

## 5. Market

- **TAM — $32B.** US sleep-tech devices and sleep-adjacent wellness (aromatherapy, sound machines, sleep-focused app subscriptions), growing double digits annually.
- **SAM — $4.2B.** US adults 18–45 who already buy sleep or meditation products online — the DTC-reachable slice of that spend.
- **SOM — $40M by Year 3.** Roughly 0.9% SAM penetration via DTC plus two national retail partners, consistent with the Year-3 projection below.

Beachhead segments, in launch order:

1. **Lucid dreamers & dream enthusiasts** — organic, evangelical, already spending effort (not yet money). CAC near zero via community content.
2. **Sleep-wellness optimizers** — the Hatch/Oura/Eight Sleep buyer; owns three sleep products and wants a fourth with a new story.
3. **Restless sleepers** — people with recurring stress dreams; marketed strictly as "end the workday-rerun dreams" wellness, not treatment.
4. **Gifting** — a $189 "give someone better dreams" gift is a wedding/holiday natural; pods are the repeatable stocking stuffer.

## 8. Go-to-market

1. **Ignite (months 0–6):** DTC only. Creator-led launch in lucid-dreaming, ASMR, and sleep-tok niches; "Design your dream" configurator as the viral shareable; waitlist drops to manufacture scarcity.
2. **Scale (months 6–18):** Paid social behind proven creative; gifting pushes for Q4; collab pod drops as recurring PR moments; app dream-cards as the organic loop (every shared dream is an ad).
3. **Retail (months 18–36):** Two national partners in the sleep/wellness set. The Halo demos beautifully on an endcap (scent + glow), and pods give retail a replenishment SKU — the thing sleep-aisle buyers ask every hardware vendor for and rarely get.

## 10. The ask

**$500,000 for 10%** — $5M valuation, ~1.7× forward revenue at the Year-1 run rate.

Use of funds:

- **45% — inventory.** First 20k-unit production run; hardware at 84% margin makes inventory the highest-ROI dollar in the plan.
- **30% — growth marketing.** Creator program + paid social behind the proven "design your dream" creative.
- **15% — app & R&D.** Dream-journal AI, cue-scheduling model, Duet mode.
- **10% — retail readiness.** Packaging, endcap demo units, EDI/ops.

Ideal Shark: retail and licensing muscle — pods are a natural licensed-IP business, and the endcap demo is the retail story.

## 11. Anticipated Shark objections — prepared answers

- **"Is this real science or a placebo diffuser?"** The two underlying mechanisms — odor cueing during sleep and targeted dream incubation — are peer-reviewed and replicated (Science 2007; MIT Media Lab 2020). We promise thematic influence and recall, not scripted dreams, and the in-app journal shows users their own hit rate. Also: even our floor case is a premium smart diffuser with an audio ritual, at diffuser margins.
- **"Hardware is hard."** This is diffuser-class hardware — $18 BOM, no display, no battery-life fight, standard cool-air atomizer supply chain. The hard part is the software and the brand, which is where the funding goes.
- **"What about churn?"** Pods are consumable and collectible (limited drops), the journal creates a streak habit, and 7% monthly churn is already in the model. Even a subscriber who lapses at month 6 delivered ~$85 of gross profit on top of a fully-paid-back acquisition.
- **"Safety/regulatory?"** IFRA-compliant fragrances, cool-air micro-dosing at a fraction of diffuser output, no heat element, auto-cutoff. Wellness positioning, zero medical claims — reviewed exactly like the aromatherapy category we ship next to.
- **"Why hasn't anyone done this?"** The science matured in the last five years, sonar-based (wearable-free) sleep staging became commodity even more recently, and everyone in sleep-tech is locked in a red-ocean fight over sleep *quality* metrics. Dreams were nobody's job. Now they're ours.
