# First-Call & Offer Script — Sales Agent Candidates

*For whoever runs candidate calls. The rule that governs everything here: **never promise anything beyond what's written in the posting and the comp one-pager.** Verbal side-deals become disputes.*

## Before the call (5 min)

- Read their screening answers. Question 5 ("how would you source your first 10 merchants?") is the tell — a concrete answer (a niche, a network, a route) predicts production; a vague one ("hard work and grinding") predicts ghosting.
- Note their industry background. Ex-ISO reps get the residual-transparency pitch; industry outsiders get more product education and a harder 90-day-ramp warning.

## Call structure (20–25 minutes)

### 1. Rapport + their story (5 min)

Ask: **"Tell me about your last sales role — and if it was payments, what made you leave?"**

Let them talk. Ex-ISO reps will usually volunteer the exact pain points we solve: shrinking splits, residual statements they couldn't verify, clawbacks, promised leads that never came. Every grievance they name is a pitch you no longer have to make — note them and mirror their words back in step 3.

### 2. The honest role description (5 min) — read this part essentially verbatim

> "Before we go further, I want to be as straight with you as we are with merchants. This is a 1099, commission-only role. No base salary, no benefits, no draw. The first 90 days are the hard part — you're building a residual base from zero, and the activation bonuses are what you eat on while it builds. If that doesn't work for your situation, this isn't your job, and I'd rather we both know now."

*(If they're out, thank them and end warmly — a respectful no builds the brand. If they're in:)*

> "Here's the actual structure: activation bonuses from $150 up to $1,000 depending on the merchant's size — a corner shop pays $150, a $100K-a-month account pays $1,000, and POS or Capital on the deal adds $100 more. Your first 90 days have Fast Start milestones worth up to $1,500 on top. Then the engine: a lifetime residual split that starts at 50% and climbs to 60 and 70 as your book grows. Residuals are never clawed back — the only clawback in the whole program is the activation bonus, and only if the merchant cancels inside 90 days. Everything pays on the 15th, and you see every merchant's numbers in your own portal."

### 3. The show, don't tell (5 min) — this is the wow moment

Screen-share, don't describe:

1. **The agent portal / residual dashboard** — "Every account you sign shows up here. Every month, its residual, your share. You never have to take our word for a number."
2. **The AI statement analyzer** — "Get a prospect's Square statement, upload it, and this builds your savings comparison and quote. This is how you close in one meeting instead of three."
3. Mention the objection-script library and cost calculator — "You're not getting handed a rate sheet and a good-luck."

This demo is the highest-leverage five minutes of the call. Tools they can see beat promises they've heard before.

### 4. Their questions (5 min) — honest answers to the predictable ones

- **"How do I know you'll actually pay me?"** → "Fair question — everyone's been burned. Two answers: the dashboard you just saw means you can verify every number yourself, and residuals are contractually never clawed back. We can't ask merchants to trust us on fees and then play games with our own agents."
- **"Do you provide leads?"** → Truthfully: "Prospecting is the job — I won't pretend otherwise. We do generate inbound and referral leads, and those go to producing agents. But if your plan depends on us feeding you appointments, this isn't the right fit."
- **"What if Delt gets acquired or shuts down?"** → Do not overpromise: "Honest answer: we're an early-stage company and I can't guarantee the future. What I can point to is the agreement — your residual terms are in writing — and the fact that our whole brand is built on telling the truth about money."
- **"Can I sell other products / keep my other line?"** → `[FOUNDER TO CONFIRM before answering — see strategy memo §7/§8]`
- **"Why is your split so high? What's the catch?"** → "No catch — the catch is that there's no salary. We'd rather pay producers a lot than pay non-producers anything. Our model only works if you win."

### 5. Close (2 min)

Qualified candidate:
> "Next steps: I'll send you the comp plan in writing — the same numbers we just discussed, including our assumptions, so check my math. If it reads right to you, we send the agent agreement, get you into the portal, and plan your first two weeks. What questions do you want answered before then?"

## Disqualify (politely) when you hear

- Needs immediate/guaranteed income, asks about a base or draw more than once after the honest description
- Expects leads to be provided; no articulable prospecting plan even when prompted
- Vague or evasive about how they'd get their first 10 accounts
- Overselling/bluster with no specifics ("I can sign 50 accounts a month") — this profile churns and burns merchants, and bad merchants cost us more than no merchants

## Offer + onboarding

1. Send the comp plan one-pager (in this folder) — never a modified or annotated version.
2. Send the Delt agent agreement `[FOUNDER TO CONFIRM: agreement must exist and be counsel-reviewed before first offer]`.
3. On signature: portal access, @delt email, Account Manager title, business cards ordered.
4. Week-one plan: product walkthrough, cash-discount objection scripts, statement-analyzer training, and their own written first-30-days prospecting plan (theirs, not assigned — assigning it is a misclassification risk; see strategy memo §7).

## What interviewers must never say

- Any earnings number not in the comp one-pager, or any number framed as "typical"
- "Guaranteed," "salary," "draw," "benefits," "leads provided," "exclusive territory"
- Anything about acquisition, buyouts, or equity
- Softening of the 90-day-ramp warning to keep a wavering candidate — the wrong hire costs more than the lost applicant
